# Cut-piece validator

`tools/cut-validate.mjs` checks a `<body>.cut.glb` + `<body>.cut.manifest.json` against `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`. It is independent of the tool that makes the files: it reads the GLB by hand (JSON chunk + BIN chunk, accessors for POSITION, NORMAL, TEXCOORD_0, JOINTS_0, WEIGHTS_0 and indices, strides and normalised integers handled) and has no dependency beyond `src/sha256.mjs`.

```
node tools/cut-validate.mjs <body.cut.glb> <body.cut.manifest.json> [--source <body.glb>] [--json] [--hash] [--plane-offset x,y,z]
node tools/cut-validate.mjs <file.glb> --hash        # only the sha256 of the bytes (determinism: run the tool twice, compare)
```

Prints a table (PASS / FAIL / SKIP per check, per-piece open-edge counts, the first violations) and exits 1 on any failed check. `--json` prints the full result. `--hash` adds the GLB's sha256 to the output. `--plane-offset` shifts the manifest planes if they are in a different frame than the GLB (the measure rows put the toes on y = 0; the GLB may not): pass the manifest's `plane_frame.bind_offset` (`docs/CUT-TOOL.md`).

```
node tools/cut-validate.mjs <plain.glb> --self [--json]
```
`--self` prints the defects a surface already has (open edges, over-used edges, flipped-winding edges, the whole GLB welded as one group): run it on the plain source to see what the cut inherits.

## Native versus introduced (watertight and orientation)

A source mesh can have open edges (hair cards, a hairline slit), and a cut that leaves them alone must not fail for them. With `--source`, the validator welds the source surface as one group, lists its defects, and sorts every defect of a piece (with its caps) into:

- **native**: the output edge lies on a source edge that has the same defect. An open or over-used output edge is native when both its ends are within 0.05 mm of one source open (or over-used) edge, so the two halves of a source edge that the cut split at a plane are native too. A flipped-winding output edge is native when it lies on a flipped source edge. An open output edge shorter than 1 mm whose two ends both lie on source open edges is native as well: it is the cap's closing chord across a hairline hole the source already had (the Minotaur's neck slit), which has no piece edge to meet.
- **introduced**: everything else (an edge the source had closed, or wound consistently, that the cut left open, over-used or flipped).

`watertight` and `orientation` **pass when no defect was introduced** and report the native count (`every piece with its caps is closed except N edge(s) native to the source (...); none introduced`; the table shows per piece `open edges (n native)`). They fail on introduced defects, and the details list those only. Without `--source` there is nothing to be native to, so every defect counts as introduced (the strict behaviour). Nothing else is relaxed: a hole the cut made in a closed surface, a cap that leaves a cut open, or a flipped cap fail as before.

## plane_exempt (hair on the head piece; rigid accessory layers)

Rigid accessory layers (`docs/CUT-BATCH.md`, row `overrides.rigid_accessory_materials`) use this same declaration on whichever piece holds them, with no change to the validator: the exempt materials are the accessory's own (a layer that shares its material with the body shell is given a copy named `<material>.rigid`, so the exemption never reaches the shell). The body shell is checked in full.

The manifest may carry `plane_exempt: { "piece_head": ["<material>", ...] }` and a non-empty `plane_exempt_reason` (Physics Dev ruling, `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`: hair goes whole to the head, re-bound to the Head bone, and hangs below the neck plane). The validator honours it for the **`planes` check only**: vertices of a primitive with a listed material are not tested against the planes. It also leaves them out of the piece centroid that the `caps` outward test uses (hair hanging below the cap would otherwise drag the head's centroid under its own neck cap). Everything else applies to those vertices: `weights`, `bone_side` (a Head-only binding is on the head's side; a weight on Neck is not), `watertight`, `orientation`, `caps` and `conservation`. `structure` rejects a `plane_exempt` with no reason, a piece that is not a `piece_*` mesh, or a material the piece does not use. The planes line says how many vertices were not checked.

## Checks

| check | what it verifies |
|---|---|
| `structure` | manifest schema `cut-pieces/1`; the seven `piece_*` and twelve `cap_*` meshes exist and match the manifest (region, `shown_when_cut`, `detached_by`); exactly one skin and every piece and cap is skinned with it; with `--source`, the skeleton joints and their parents are unchanged |
| `watertight` | each piece plus its caps: every undirected edge is used by exactly two triangles, vertices welded by position within 1e-5 so UV or normal seams are not holes. Reports open-edge and over-used-edge counts per piece (with `--source`: how many are native to the source) and the world position of the first three introduced edges. Passes when the cut introduced none (see below) |
| `orientation` | the two triangles on a shared edge traverse it in opposite directions (no flipped winding); native flipped edges are reported, introduced ones fail |
| `weights` | every vertex sums to 1 within 1e-3, no NaN or negative, joints inside the skin, at most 4 influences (no weight in `WEIGHTS_1`) |
| `bone_side` | no vertex of a piece or its caps is bound to a bone on the other side of a cut. The side sets come from the skeleton tree and the manifest planes: a bone is on the positive side of a plane when it is at or beyond it and its nearest joint ancestor is not; that bone and its descendants are the positive subtree (neck: Head and below; shoulder: the arm; hip: the leg; waist: Spine and everything above). A region may use only bones on its own side of every plane it touches |
| `caps` | each cap has triangles, `TEXCOORD_0`, `NORMAL`; the mean normal points from the piece centroid toward the cap centroid; the winding agrees with the stored normals and faces outward |
| `conservation` | needs `--source`: sum of piece triangle areas (caps excluded) within 1 percent of the source, bounding box equal (1e-4), every welded source vertex present among the pieces' vertices. Skipped, not failed, without `--source` |
| `planes` | every vertex of a piece and its caps is on its side of its manifest planes within 1e-4 (no overlap across a cut); vertices of a manifest `plane_exempt` material are skipped (below) |

### Optional cuts (manifest `optional_units`; `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`)

When the manifest enables `head_split`, `belly_open` or `hands_feet` the validator derives its tables from it instead of the fixed seven and twelve, and appends one check per enabled cut. With neither enabled the output is the eight checks above, unchanged.

- **`head_split`** (the tables become: `piece_head` replaced by `piece_head_left` / `piece_head_right`, both `detached_by` `head` and `separated_by` `head_split`; `cap_neck_head` replaced by `cap_neck_head_left` / `_right`; plus `cap_split_head_left` / `_right`; `structure` checks all of it, and fails a manifest that lists both `head` and its halves). Everything else applies to the halves as to any piece: `watertight` and `orientation` (each half with its two caps), `weights`, `bone_side` (the sagittal plane has no bone side; both halves use the head's bones, the `neck` + side), `caps` (outward, winding agrees), `conservation` (the halves' area is in the total), `planes` (left on the + side of `optional_planes.head_split`, right on the - side, hair `plane_exempt` per half). The extra check **`head_split`**: the plane is horizontal (the body's left-right axis); each half really has vertices strictly on its side; each cut face (`cap_split_head_*`) is flat on the plane (1e-4), every triangle winds away from its half (left: -n, right: +n) and its stored normals agree; **conservation of area**: the two cut faces are the same section seen from each side, areas within 1 percent; the **seam**: every welded vertex of one half on the plane has a partner in the other half, so the halves meet exactly.
- **`belly_open`** (`cap_belly_opening` joins `parts` as a decal: it is an open surface by design, so `watertight`, `orientation` and `caps` skip it, while `weights`, `bone_side` (upper-torso bones only) and `planes` (above the waist plane, inside neck and shoulders) apply to it; `structure` checks its manifest entry (`upper_torso`, `belly_open`) and its mesh). The extra check **`belly_open`**: the manifest says `removes_geometry: false` and `detaches: null`; the frame is orthonormal; every triangle's winding and the stored normals face forward; every vertex is inside the declared ellipse; every vertex is in front of the torso skin (the front-most triangle of `piece_upper_torso` along the same ray) by the declared lift, between `rim_lift` and `rim_lift + bulge` (0.2 mm tolerance), so it neither sinks into the skin nor floats; patch area between 1 and 1.6 times the ellipse area and equal to the manifest's number; both materials present. That the torso itself lost nothing is `conservation` (all pieces' area equals the source).

- **`hands_feet`** (the tables become: eleven pieces, `piece_hand_*` / `piece_foot_*` added after their arm and leg, each `detached_by` its own id; twenty caps, the eight `cap_wrist_*` / `cap_ankle_*` with the unit that shows them; the arm and leg pieces gain the constraint "- side" of their wrist or ankle plane, the hand and foot pieces "+ side" of it and of the shoulder or hip plane they hang from; the four planes come from `optional_planes`). `structure` also fails a manifest that does not enable `hands_feet` while the GLB holds `piece_hand_*` / `piece_foot_*`, and a missing `optional_planes` entry. Everything else applies to the new pieces as to any: `watertight` and `orientation` (each piece with its caps, per piece, no tolerance relaxed), `weights`, `bone_side` (a wrist or ankle plane's + set is the skeleton subtree that crosses it, restricted to the limb it belongs to: Hand and fingers, Foot and toes; so a hand vertex on the ForeArm, or an arm vertex on the Hand, fails with the piece, the bone and the plane named), `caps`, `conservation` (the new pieces' area is in the total), `planes` (every vertex on its side of its plane within 1e-4). The extra check **`hands_feet`**, per plane: the normal is along the bone (cosine at least 0.999 of the skeleton's ForeArm to Hand or Leg to Foot direction, by joint name; a rig without those names is reported in the table, not failed) and the Hand or Foot joint lies between 0 and one bone length beyond the plane; both pieces have vertices strictly on their side; both cut faces are flat on the plane (1e-4), wound away from their own piece (end piece -n, limb piece +n) with agreeing stored normals; the two cut faces have equal area (1 percent); the **seam**: every welded vertex of one piece on the plane has a partner in the other, so the pieces meet exactly. A body that fails says FAIL with the plane and the numbers (the table lines list the areas, vertex counts and joint offsets); nothing is loosened.

Plane keys in the manifest are matched loosely (`neck`, `waist`, `shoulder_left` / `shoulderL` / `shoulder_right`, `hip_left` ...). Region sides: head is beyond the neck plane; upper torso is below the neck, above the waist and inside both shoulder planes; root is below the waist and above both hip planes; an arm is beyond its shoulder plane; a leg is beyond its hip plane.

## Tests

`test/cut-validate.test.mjs` builds a stack of boxes on an 8-joint skeleton (Hips, Spine, Neck, Head, two arms, two legs: eight, not five, because seven pieces need both arms and both legs) with seven pieces and twelve caps generated in the test, a valid variant that passes, and one broken variant per check: a hole (`watertight`), a weight sum off (`weights`), a head vertex on the Neck bone (`bone_side`), an inward cap normal (`caps`), a plane the head overlaps (`planes`), a missing cap mesh (`structure`), plus a missing source area (`conservation`) and the CLI exit codes. Cases for the hair and edge rules: a hair card with its own material across the neck plane (passes with `plane_exempt`, fails `planes` without it, still fails `bone_side` when half its weight is on Neck, does not excuse the head's own surface across the plane, `plane_exempt` without a reason or naming an unknown piece or material fails `structure`); a hole the source has too (native: passes with `--source`, fails without it), an introduced hole (fails), a native open edge the cut split in two halves (native), a flipped triangle the source has too (native) and one the cut made (fails `orientation`), and `surfaceReport` / `--self`. Run on the VPS: `node --test test/cut-validate.test.mjs`. The `hands_feet` check is tested in `test/cut-units.test.mjs` on the same synthetic body (11 pieces and 20 caps, flat caps with the section area on both sides, watertight pieces, bone sides, the combination with head split, hair, a rigid skirt layer and the tilted shoulder plane; broken manifests: a plane moved into the forearm fails `planes` naming the plane and the worst distance, a tilted ankle plane, a missing cap or plane, a wrong unit, `detached_by` or a plain manifest over a hands_feet GLB fail `structure`; a hand vertex bound to the ForeArm fails `bone_side`) and in `test/cut-batch.test.mjs` (the batch pass-through). The optional-cut checks are tested in `test/cut-units.test.mjs` on the synthetic skinned body (the cut tool makes the files the validator reads): both halves and the cut faces on the plane (area of the face = column section plus a hollow tube's two walls), the belly patch against the plain cut (every piece and cap equal, so nothing removed), and one broken manifest per rule (plane moved off the midline fails `planes`, a missing cut-face cap or `separated_by` or a wrong unit fails `structure`, an ellipse half the size, a lift declared differently, `removes_geometry: true` fail `belly_open`).
