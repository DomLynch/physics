# tools/cut-units.mjs: one body GLB into seven pieces and twelve caps

The mesh pipeline behind `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`. It reads a skinned humanoid GLB, its measure row and its rig map, and writes `<body>.cut.glb` and `<body>.cut.manifest.json`: seven `piece_*` skinned meshes and twelve `cap_*` skinned meshes on the unchanged skeleton, the original materials plus `cap_flesh`. Rendering and the runtime (`createCutRig`) are not part of this tool.

## Run

```
node tools/cut-units.mjs <body.glb> <body.measure.json> <rig-map.json> <out-dir> [--body name] [--debug] [--head-split] [--belly-open] [--hands-feet]
node tools/cut-units.mjs minotaur-106.glb data/bodies/minotaur-106.measure.json data/rig-maps/mixamo-106.json out/
```

Needs `@gltf-transform/core`, `@gltf-transform/extensions` and `meshoptimizer` (devDependencies: the V3 106 bodies are meshopt-compressed and webp-textured, and the tool reads and writes through gltf-transform; nothing in the package's runtime loads this file). Runs in about two seconds per body. `--body` names the output files and the manifest `body` (default: the measure row's `id`). `--debug` prints which fragments the bones moved, the cap loops per region and the re-bind counts. Same input, same output bytes (no clocks, no random, every map ordered).

Many bodies at once: `node tools/cut-batch.mjs` runs measure, cut and validate over the rows of `data/cut-bodies.json` and writes a receipt (`docs/CUT-BATCH.md`). `opts` also takes `planes` (fractions along the joint pairs, `PLANE_ROWS`) and `homeMinWeight` (the 20 percent bone rule), both rows in the batch file.

`opts` of `cutGlb(bytes, row, rigMap, opts)` (the function the CLI wraps): `body`, `rig`, `source` (manifest strings), `debug`, `crotchPlanes: false` (plain thigh-normal hip planes), `hairMaterials` (exact material names that go whole to the head; default: any name matching `/hair|lash|brow/i`; `[]` turns it off), `rigidAccessoryMaterials` (layers kept whole, one piece, one bone: `docs/CUT-BATCH.md` "Rigid accessory layers"; `--debug` lists the shells of every `shells_inside` match as `report.accessory_shells`), `planeSnap` (metres, the snap onto a plane), `headSplit`, `bellyOpen`, `handsFeet` (opt-in cuts, below).

The output keeps the animations, textures and node names of the source. `<body>.cut.glb` is the contract file: **uncompressed and un-quantised**, plain float32 POSITION / NORMAL / TEXCOORD_0 in **metres** (the source's dequantisation matrix is baked into the vertices and out of the inverse binds), uint16 JOINTS_0, float32 WEIGHTS_0, uint16/32 indices, so plain readers (tools/cut-validate.mjs) work. `--meshopt` also writes `<body>.cut.min.glb` (meshopt + quantised, for shipping, not the contract file). `--source-out <file>` writes the source in the same plain form (needed by `cut-validate --source` when the source is a meshopt web GLB):

```
node tools/cut-units.mjs src.glb row.json rig.json out/ --source-out out/src-plain.glb
node tools/cut-validate.mjs out/body.cut.glb out/body.cut.manifest.json --source out/src-plain.glb --plane-offset <manifest plane_frame.bind_offset>
```
The manifest's plane points are in the measure row's frame; the validator needs `--plane-offset` = `plane_frame.bind_offset` to compare them with the GLB. The internal hip bisector is listed under `internal_planes`, not `planes`.

## Method

1. **Frames.** The measure row is floor-lifted (`tools/glb-measure.mjs` puts the toes on y = 0). The tool recovers that offset from the 20 role joints (refusing a row that does not fit the GLB beyond 2 mm) and works in the GLB's bind-pose world. Quantised bodies (gltfpack) keep their vertices in a normalised space and bake the dequantisation into the inverse binds; the tool finds that matrix (`jointWorld x inverseBind`, the same for every joint) and does all geometry in metres, writing positions back in the stored space. The manifest carries `plane_frame.vertex_to_world`, `bind_offset` and `crotch_apex`. Plane points in the manifest are in the measure row's frame.
2. **Planes** (contract start values, with two refinements, below). Neck: half way Neck to Head, normal along Neck to Head. Waist: the Spine joint, normal Hips to Spine. Shoulders: the Arm joint. Hips: the UpLeg joint.
3. **Split.** Every triangle is cut at every plane it crosses (Sutherland-Hodgman). A new vertex on an edge gets position, normal, tangent (if present), every UV set, any other float attribute, and all skin weights interpolated; it is created once per edge and plane (looked up by the edge's two end positions, so UV-seam duplicates of one edge get bit-identical split points) and shared by every triangle on that edge. A vertex within 0.09 mm (5e-5 of the height) of a plane counts as on it, so no sliver triangles. Never assigned whole: no gap and no ledge.
4. **Fragments and regions.** Cutting gives cells (the sign pattern over the planes). Within a cell, connected pieces of mesh (welded by position) are fragments. A fragment goes to the region its cell names (head above the neck plane, an arm beyond its shoulder plane, a leg below its hip plane, upper torso above the waist, root otherwise; priority head, arms, legs, torso). **Unless its bones disagree:** when the cell's region owns under 20 percent of the fragment's skin weight, the feasible region owning most of it takes the fragment. "Feasible" means the cell's planes allow it (head needs the + side of the neck plane; an arm or leg the + side of its own plane; the torso the - side of neck and shoulders and the + side of the waist; the root the - side of waist and both hips), so every piece vertex stays on its side of every plane that bounds its piece (`cut-validate` check 8). This is what makes the planes safe on a real body, where an infinite plane also crosses unrelated mesh: a foot beyond the shoulder plane's far side stays in its leg, a tail across the hip bisector stays in the root. Other gear below a plane that its bones put elsewhere goes to the piece the plane says, and its Head weights are re-bound to that piece's bones. **Hair is the exception** (Physics Dev ruling, `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`): every triangle of a hair-card, eyelash or brow material (name matches `/hair|lash|brow/i`, or `opts.hairMaterials`: a list of exact names, `[]` for none) is taken out before any splitting, so it is **never cut**, goes whole to `piece_head`, and every vertex is bound to the Head bone alone (weight 1, rigid). The manifest declares it (`plane_exempt`, `plane_exempt_reason`, stats `hair_triangles`, `hair_vertices`). Hair is also left out of the cap analysis (its open card edges are not on a plane loop). Where two fragments of one region meet across a plane, the shared split vertices make them one surface again, so no cap appears there.
5. **Bone ownership and re-bind.** Each bone belongs to the region of its nearest ancestor-or-self among Hips (root), Spine (upper torso: Spine, Spine1, Spine2, Neck, shoulders), Head (head, hair, jaw...), LeftArm / RightArm (arm), LeftUpLeg / RightUpLeg (leg, feet, toes, twist bones). A piece keeps weights on its own bones and moves every other weight to the nearest bone it does own (tree distance; Neck goes to Head in the head piece, Head goes to Neck in the torso piece, Hips goes to UpLeg in a leg), merges duplicates, keeps the four largest, renormalises to 1. `orphan_weights` counts influences left on a foreign bone after this: 0 on both bodies.
6. **Caps.** For each piece, the boundary edges that lie on a cut plane are chained into loops; a loop along two cap planes (the root's lower edge where the two hip planes meet) is cut at the two points where it changes plane and each half closed by a straight chord, so every cap is flat. Loops are triangulated by **ear clipping with hole bridging** (outer rings and holes by orientation and containment, so a hollow tube or a horn crossing the plane gives a ring with a hole), keeping every loop vertex (a dropped collinear vertex would leave a T-junction against the piece). Cap triangles are wound to meet the piece's edges the other way round, so the outward normal comes out by construction for every loop that does not cross itself. **A loop that crosses itself** (the plane cuts a folded or overlapping layer of the source mesh: Psyche's waist has a section loop of 178 points that crosses itself 8 times, leaving three lobes of opposite winding of 0.03 to 0.1 square millimetres) has regions of negative winding that no consistent triangulation can cover with outward triangles: ear clipping is forced to emit a few triangles against the plane's outward direction (forced ears, `cap_forced_ears`). Those triangles are kept (the cap must stay closed and oriented with its piece: dropping or flipping them would leave open or flipped edges), and each one gets **its own three vertices carrying the normal of its winding**, so every cap triangle's winding agrees with its stored normals (`cut-validate` check 6). `cap_inward_triangles` counts them (Psyche: 5 per waist cap, 10 in all; the Minotaur and the synthetic body: 0). Cap vertices copy the loop vertices' position and weights, UV is a planar fit 0..1, normal is the outward plane normal.
7. **Native mesh flaws.** A cut loop that meets a hairline slit in the source is bridged across gaps under 1 mm (`cap_gaps_bridged`). Source vertices are never moved (an opt-in `snapCracks: true` merges vertices closer than 0.15 mm; off because the validator wants every source vertex position kept). Open edges the source already had are counted separately (`open_edges_native`, against `source_open_edges`).

## Optional cuts: `--head-split`, `--belly-open` (default off; `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`)

```
node tools/cut-units.mjs src.glb row.json rig.json out/ --head-split --belly-open
```
`opts.headSplit` / `opts.bellyOpen` are `true` or an object that overrides fields of the table row (`HEAD_SPLIT`, `BELLY_OPEN`, exported: plane fractions, ellipse size, tessellation, lift; the region and cap names live in the same rows). Off, nothing below runs and the output is the plain contract byte for byte.

**Head split.** The plane (`buildHeadSplitPlane`): normal = the left-right axis (LeftArm minus RightArm and LeftUpLeg minus RightUpLeg, vertical part dropped, averaged), point = the Neck to Head joint line at `point_frac` (0.5), moved onto the symmetry plane (the mean of the four joints along that axis). After the normal arrangement has decided what is head, the head's triangles are split once more at this plane with the same machinery as the others (shared split vertices, every attribute and skin weight interpolated), giving `head_left` (+ side) and `head_right`. Hair is not cut: each connected hair card goes whole to the half holding most of its area (signed area-weighted distance of its triangle centres), still Head-only bound. The cap code is unchanged: a half's boundary lies on the neck plane and the sagittal plane, and the loop-splitting that closes the root's two hip planes by chords is generalised to any even number of alternating runs of two planes (the ends of each plane's runs are sorted along the planes' meeting line and paired, so a hollow tube or a horn crossing both planes gives its half rings and wall strips instead of one folded polygon; two runs behave exactly as before). Each half gets `cap_neck_head_*` and `cap_split_head_*`. The sagittal plane only counts for the halves: other pieces' native open edges on the midline are not mistaken for a cut. Bone side: a half uses the bones of the head region (`bones: "head"` in the row), so both halves are re-bound exactly as `piece_head` was.

**Belly opening.** Frame: forward = Foot to ToeBase (both feet, made square to lateral), lateral as above, up = the waist-to-neck direction made square to both. Ellipse centre at `centre_frac` (0.28) of the way from the waist plane point to the neck plane point, on the symmetry plane; half axes `half_height_frac` (0.18) of that distance and `half_width_frac` (0.3) of the shoulder span. The tool refuses (with the number) an ellipse that reaches under `min_clearance_frac` (0.08) of waist-to-neck above the waist plane (so every vertex stays on the torso's side of the waist plane), and an ellipse that leaves the torso (a ray with no skin behind it). A polar grid of `rings` x `segments` (6 x 32) is projected along -forward onto the **front-most** triangle of `piece_upper_torso` at each point and lifted `rim_lift + bulge * (1 - s^2)` (1.5 mm at the rim, 9.5 mm at the centre) along forward, `s` being the radius fraction; position, hit normal (for lighting), UV (planar ellipse fit) and weights (the hit triangle's three vertices' own-side bone weights, barycentric, four largest renormalised) come from the hit. The rings up to `core_frac` are the `cap_inner` primitive, the rest the `cap_flesh` primitive (one mesh, two primitives; the ring where they meet is stored in both). Triangles are wound to face forward. Nothing in the torso changes. To tune it per body, change the row (a body with a long torso wants a lower `centre_frac`).

Stats (Minotaur-106 / Psyche-106, `--head-split --belly-open`): head halves 3,479 + 3,517 vertices (Minotaur), 3,054 + 2,955 non-hair (Psyche, hair 5,159 + 4,189 vertices whole), cut face 0.0489 m2 (left and right within 0.02 percent) and 0.0394 m2, seam 231 / 196 welded vertices on the plane in each half, belly patch 225 vertices, 352 triangles, 1.12 / 1.13 times its ellipse area; orphan weights 0; total piece area equals the source (0.000 percent).

## Optional cut: `--hands-feet` (default off; `docs/CUT-CONTRACT.md`)

```
node tools/cut-units.mjs src.glb row.json rig.json out/ --hands-feet
```
`opts.handsFeet` is `true` or an object that overrides fields of the table row `HANDS_FEET` (exported: `wrist_frac`, `ankle_frac`, the `limbs` table with plane, joints and piece names, the eight `caps`). Off, nothing below runs and every cut GLB and manifest is byte for byte what it was (the published Minotaur-106, Psyche-106, Gargantua-106 and Arachne-106 rows regenerate to their pinned sha256).

**Planes** (`buildHandsFeetPlanes`). Wrist: through the Hand joint (`wrist_frac` of the way back toward the ForeArm joint, start 0), normal ForeArm to Hand. Ankle: through the Foot joint (`ankle_frac` toward the Leg joint, start 0), normal Leg to Foot. The + side is the hand or the foot. **Split.** After the normal arrangement has decided the arm and leg pieces (so every rule above, including the 20 percent bone rule, sees the plain seven regions: the hand and foot bones count as their arm and leg there), each limb piece's triangles are cut once more at its plane with the same machinery as the other planes (shared split vertices, every attribute and skin weight interpolated); the + side becomes `piece_hand_*` / `piece_foot_*`. **Bones.** The Hand and Foot joints anchor the new regions (`rig map` roles `LeftHand`, `RightHand`, `LeftFoot`, `RightFoot` must be skin joints), so the Hand bone and its descendants (fingers) belong to the hand, the Foot bone and the toes to the foot; the arm and leg pieces move those weights to the nearest bone they own (ForeArm, Leg), the hand and foot move theirs the other way (Hand, Foot), with the same re-bind rule and the four-largest cut as at the other planes. **Caps.** The cap code is unchanged: a limb piece and its end piece each have a boundary on the plane, chained into loops (several loops where fingers or toes cross, holes by bridging) and triangulated; eight caps `cap_wrist_*_hand|arm`, `cap_ankle_*_foot|leg`. **Interactions.** `shoulder_mode: "tilt"` and rigid accessory layers see the plain seven regions and are unchanged; an accessory placed in an arm or leg stays whole in that piece and is bound to a bone the limb piece owns. It combines with `--head-split` and `--belly-open`.

**Real bodies (V3 106 rig, 2026-10-30).** Minotaur-106 (with head split and belly open), Gargantua-106 and Arachne-106 pass all checks with `--hands-feet` (11 checks on the Minotaur, 9 on the others; Arachne's 6,925 open edges are the hair cards the source already has). Psyche-106 FAILS at the default `ankle_frac` 0: the source mesh has a native slit on the ankle plane (a gap of about 2.5 mm that the 1 mm bridge does not close, 19 open cap chains, 39 open edges at the cuts) and `watertight` and `hands_feet` report it with the numbers; it passes with `ankle_frac` 0.1 or 0.2 (4 open edges at the cuts, all native). No tolerance is relaxed for it.

## Refinements of the contract's plane values (and why)

- **Shoulder planes are vertical.** The normal is the upper-arm bone with its vertical part dropped. A plane square to a slightly drooping arm (the Minotaur's arm points 10 degrees down) tilts into the flank and hands the arm a slab of ribs: with the bone normal the Minotaur's arm piece started at y 1.185 m, with the vertical plane at 1.27 m.
- **Hip planes pass through the crotch.** Left and right hip planes meet along the midline. With the thigh-normal planes the wedge below both planes held the crotch skin, so the leg pieces needed a wall between them (a non-planar, folded cap). The tool finds the crotch apex (scanning down from the hip joint for the height where the left and right legs' innermost bone-weighted vertices reach the midline: Minotaur y 0.8174 in the row frame, Psyche 0.8517) and turns each hip plane about the UpLeg joint the least that puts it through the apex (normal about 37 degrees off the thigh on the Minotaur). The wedge is empty, the leg caps are flat, and no extra plane is needed (an internal `hip_split` bisector stays in the manifest, flagged `internal`, for stray mesh; it has no cap). `crotchPlanes: false` gives the plain contract planes.
- **Parallel thighs: one hip plane and a midline wall cap.** When the two hip normals differ by under `HIP_SPLIT_PARALLEL` (0.1), `hip_split` is the midline plane and `hip_left` / `hip_right` are one shared plane (mean thigh direction, through the midpoint of the hip points, raised until both hip points are on it or below). Two named planes and four hip caps remain: the root's lower face is shared out between its two caps by the side of the midline. Mesh that crosses the midline below the plane (a belly apron, a crotch) is cut by `hip_split`; each such loop, or the part of a loop that runs along the midline, is closed by a wall cap (triangles of the leg's hip cap, flat on the midline, facing the other leg). A loop cut by more than two planes (a collar island across the neck and both shoulder planes) is chained plane by plane. Cap triangles whose winding, read from the float32 positions in the file, disagrees with their stored normals (float32 slivers, or folded triangles the float64 test did not catch; any size) get vertices of their own carrying the normal of their winding, only when more than 1 percent of the cap's triangles disagree (the validator's allowance), so every cap that passed before is byte-identical. Big Vegas (`big-vegas-106`) passes all 8 checks with this.
- Neck and waist are as the contract says.

## Limits (plain)

- **The Minotaur's neck slit.** The source mesh has a hairline hole (0.09 mm wide, 1.5 mm long) where the neck plane crosses it. The loop is bridged and capped; the hole's two edges stay open on each side. Not visible at any scale a phone shows.
- **Psyche's hair.** Her hair is open geometry (cards, one material, `Ch22_hair`) hanging to her knees. By the Physics Dev ruling it goes whole to `piece_head`, uncut and bound to Head only (method step 4), so it no longer produces open cuts, flipped edges or cap loops in the other pieces. It lies across the neck plane by design: the manifest's `plane_exempt` says so and `cut-validate` skips the planes check for those vertices only. Its open card edges (7,413 in the source) stay open and are reported as native. Consequence at runtime: when the head is cut off, the hair travels with it rigidly (no Neck, Spine or hair-bone weights), and while the head is attached the hair no longer sways with its own bones, it follows the Head bone; if hair physics is wanted later, it needs its own plan.
- **Folded cap triangles.** Psyche's waist section loop crosses itself (the source mesh overlaps itself there), so each waist cap has 5 triangles whose geometric winding is against the outward normal; they carry flipped normals (method step 6). The five triangles of a cap cover about 0.5 square centimetres, 0.2 percent of it.
- **Seams while intact.** Region meshes are always visible and each piece is bound only to its own side's bones, so at a seam where the two bones rotate relative to each other (a raised arm, a turned head) the two pieces no longer share a skinning blend and a hairline gap can open on one side of the seam. That is the contract's re-bind rule (it is what makes the cut piece not stretch); it is smallest at the neck (mid-neck plane, small relative rotation) and largest at the shoulder. Check the 8-angle render at an extreme pose before shipping a body; the fix, if needed, is a runtime one (a thin overlap skirt on the cap, or the cap shown during violent poses), not a mesh one.
- **Four influences.** Interpolated vertices and re-bound vertices keep the four largest weights (renormalised): 12 vertices on the Minotaur and 7 on Psyche lost a fifth influence. `JOINTS_1` (more than four influences per vertex) and morph targets are refused with a clear error.
- **One skin.** A second skin with the same joints (Psyche's eyelashes) is merged into the first; a skin with a joint the first lacks is refused.
- **Region by plane and bones, not by hand.** A body whose limbs cross the infinite planes in ways the bones do not explain (arms down in an A-pose with hands below the hip plane) is handled by the bone rule, not verified; the two bodies here are T-pose. Run `--debug` on a new body and read `reassigned`.
- **Extra splits.** Planes are infinite, so a triangle far from the cut but crossed by a plane (the Minotaur's feet beyond the shoulder plane, hair strands across the hip plane) is split and then re-joined in one region: harmless, but it adds vertices (Psyche 34,164 to 42,913, mostly her hair).
- **No `cap_inner`.** Only `cap_flesh` is written; a data row can still pick colours at runtime.
- **Textures** are copied as they are; caps use their own flesh material and no texture.

## Tests

`test/cut-units.test.mjs` (run on the VPS; `npm test` includes it). A synthetic skinned body (a column across the waist and neck planes, two strands and a hollow square tube across the neck plane so the cut has several loops and a hole, two legs across the hip planes, two arms across the shoulder planes in a second material, an eyelash mesh with its own skin) with floor-lifted measure row. Variants add an open hair card (own material, spine-chain weights, from above the neck plane down across the waist and hip planes) and a prism with a bow-tie section that folds through itself, so the neck cut loop crosses itself. Hair tests: the card and the eyelashes are in `piece_head` only, with their triangle and vertex counts unchanged (never cut), every vertex Head-only weight 1, `plane_exempt` and its reason in the manifest, area conserved, and the validator passes (and fails the planes check without the manifest entry). Cap winding test: with the folding prism every piece with its caps stays closed and consistently oriented, every cap triangle's winding agrees with its stored normals, the folded slivers carry the flipped normals and are counted in `cap_inward_triangles`. Asserts: manifest valid against the contract; one skin, 7 + 12 skinned meshes, materials kept; surface area conserved to 1e-9 and every vertex away from a plane in exactly one piece; every piece with its caps watertight (each welded edge shared by exactly two triangles, no degenerate triangle); no vertex bound across a cut by bone name; cap triangles and normals outward and cap areas equal the section areas (column + strands + tube annulus); determinism (same bytes twice); the CLI; a mismatched measure row refused; the triangulator on a hole and on collinear boundary vertices. When `/root/bodies/{minotaur,psyche}-106.glb` (or `CUT_BODIES_DIR`) exist the same checks that make sense run on the real bodies; the GLBs are not in the repo (licence).
